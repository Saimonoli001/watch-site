TECHNOLOGY IMPACT IN WATCHMAKING

About:

Over the years, the watch industry has evolved significantly due to technological advancements. Quartz movements have made watches more accurate, while smartwatches have added features like fitness tracking and health monitoring. Advanced machinery and computer-aided design(CAD) have made it possible to create accurate parts and improving reliability of watch.

The rise of smartwatches has also changed how people use watches. Materials such as ceramics, titanium, and carbon fiber have made watches lighter, stronger and more resistant to damage. Automated production processes have made watch manufacturing more faster and more consistent, loweing costs and improving quality. New power sources like solar and kinetic energy, have reduced the need for battery replacements,while quartz movements provide accurate timekeeping.

The history of watches is a fascinating journey of innovations and craftmanship that spans centuries. Timekeeping began with ancient methods such as sundials and water clocks, which were used by early civilization to measure the passage of time. In the 1300s, large mechanical clocks, primarily found in churches and towers, marked a significant advancement in accuracy and reliability. By the 1500s, portable timepieces began to emerge with the invention of the mainspring, allowing for the development of early watches. These small, decorative clocks were often worn as pendants and later evolved into pocket watches in the 1600s, becoming a status symbol for wealthy individuals despite their limited accuracy.

The 1700s and 1800s saw significant improvements in watchmaking. The introduction of the balance spring in 1657 greatly enhanced accuracy, while minute hands were added to improve functionality. During the 19th century, American companies like Waltham and Elgin revolutionized watch production by mass-manufacturing pocke watches, making them more accessible to the general public.
